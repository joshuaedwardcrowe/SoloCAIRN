# Stakeholder Interviews

Eight interviews with quiz hosts, three with venue managers. Interviews ran 30 to 45 minutes, recorded with permission, notes cleaned below. Identifying details changed.

## Host interviews

### H1: Experienced host, runs four venues a week

- "I spend more time marking than hosting. On a busy night, that means I'm quiet for a solid half hour out of a two-hour quiz."
- "People lose interest in the last round if they think they can't win. They start drinking more, which the venue likes, but they stop playing."
- "I have about 400 questions in rotation. I know the regulars are starting to recognise them."

### H2: New host, 6 months in

- "The worst part is the adding up. I dread the maths round because I know I'll be doing sums for ten minutes."
- "I'd love to see how teams are doing live. I think it would make the quiz feel more alive."
- "I don't want anything that makes it harder for the old couple in the corner to play with paper."

### H3: Freelance, charges per venue

- "I write my own quizzes because that's my differentiator. I wouldn't want a tool that took that from me, but I'd use a tool that let me deliver my quiz faster."
- "I track scores on a spreadsheet I built. It's a mess, but it's mine."
- "Most venues I work with have patchy wifi. That's my biggest tech worry."

### H4: Host at a chain venue

- "Our chain has a standard quiz each week, sent from head office. I just run it."
- "I don't really care about flexibility. I care about not dropping the ball in front of 80 people."
- "If the tech breaks, I need to fall back to paper in under a minute."

### H5 to H8

Summary of five further interviews: all five echoed the marking pain. Four emphasised that players not knowing their position was a bigger engagement killer than they had realised before being asked about it directly. Two were explicitly sceptical about tech at the table ("phones kill the vibe").

## Venue manager interviews

### V1: Manager of a 120-cover gastropub

- "Quiz night is our slowest growing night. I'd love to try something new without losing the regulars who expect the current format."
- "Wifi is fine in the main room. The back snug is a dead zone."
- "I don't care about the app. I care about bums on seats and food orders."

### V2: Manager of a chain pub

- "Anything we roll out has to work at all 40 venues or it's not happening."
- "Price matters. We wouldn't buy hardware. We'd expect the host to use their own laptop and whatever display we already have."

### V3: Manager of a small independent

- "Our quiz is the best night of the week. The host is the reason. If the tool makes the host look bad, we don't want it."
- "We have an old smart TV in the back room. That's our 'big screen.'"

## Themes noted at time of interview

- Marking is universally painful. Real-time scoring is a clear win.
- Engagement-from-visibility is a bigger deal than players or hosts first articulate, but lands hard once they hear it described.
- Wifi is the single most-mentioned risk.
- Hosts vary dramatically in how much they want to customise. Some write their own, some want to run whatever they are given.
- Fallback to paper must be fast and non-embarrassing.
- Players with paper and players with phones have to coexist on the same quiz.
