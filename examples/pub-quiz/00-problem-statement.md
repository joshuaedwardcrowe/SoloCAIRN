# Problem Statement: Pub Quiz Live Scoring

## What is wrong today

Pubs and small venues run weekly quiz nights using paper scoresheets and a host with a microphone. The experience has not changed in twenty years. In interviews with eight quiz hosts across five venues, three problems came up repeatedly:

1. **Scoring takes the mood out of the evening.** The host stops the quiz every round to collect, mark, and total sheets. A typical quiz has four of these pauses, each five to ten minutes long.
2. **Players cannot see their progress.** Teams know they got most answers right, but do not know where they stand versus the room. Engagement drops in the later rounds.
3. **Hosts cannot easily vary the quiz.** Building a new quiz means preparing questions, printing sheets, and buying rounds from a pub's budget. Most venues recycle quizzes more often than they want to admit.

## Who is affected

- **Quiz hosts**, who are often venue staff doing this as a side duty or freelancers running circuits of three to five venues per week.
- **Venue managers**, who care about repeat custom and the length of the average visit.
- **Players**, typically groups of 3 to 6, in the 25 to 55 age range, who come for the social experience.

## Evidence

- Eight stakeholder interviews (see `01-stakeholder-interviews.md`).
- Observation of three quiz nights at three different venues.
- Survey of 50 players via quiz hosts, with 34 responses. Notable: 78% said "not knowing our score until the end" was their top frustration.

## What "solved" looks like

- Scoring happens automatically and in real time. No mid-quiz pauses to mark sheets.
- Players see their team's position after every question.
- Hosts can run quizzes without preparing paper materials.
- The technology is invisible to players who do not want to use it; they can still play with pen and paper alongside teams using phones.

## Out of scope for this problem

- Running entire quiz leagues across many venues.
- Monetising the quizzes beyond what venues already charge.
- Live video or streaming features.
- Multiplayer quizzes across multiple venues simultaneously.

## Open questions

- Is the pain equally strong at higher-end venues versus casual pubs?
- How much of this pain is actually about scoring versus about engagement more broadly?
- Would hosts pay, or do venues need to subsidise the tooling?

## Not yet decided

The team agrees this problem is worth investigating. We have not yet committed to building. Commitment happens at the Scope PR stage.
